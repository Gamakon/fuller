\begin{abstract}
Symbolic regression (SR) searches for a closed-form expression that fits data.
In classical Gene Expression Programming (GEP) every SR operator consumes and
produces one real type, so nothing in the representation separates a physical
law from a tower of nested transcendentals that fits the training rows. This
paper describes the SR kingdom of a multi-typed GEP engine and Andrew Morgan's
Typed Symbolic Regression (TSR), in which the type of a subexpression records
how many transcendental functions lie on its deepest path. Three depth-typed
layers, \ty{F}, \ty{T1} and \ty{T2}, are enough for every one of the 128
parseable SRBench ground-truth laws, and a transcendental applied to a \ty{T2}
value has no row in the symbol table, so it cannot be expressed. We describe the
real-domain operator set, its protected variants, the rewriting system (fuller)
that edits individuals during the search through equality saturation, the
fitness (linear scaling, six error objectives and the hyperspherical TrueNorth
angle, HFF), and where each stage runs. We then report the measured results,
each with its protocol. On one development seed with 90~s a problem and
SRBench's symbolic check as the stop condition, TSR recovered 74 of 133 laws,
and 79 after fuller's post-search candidate generation. In the only
same-engine comparison of typed and untyped search (one development seed,
90~s, an RTX 4090), TSR certified 72 laws against the untyped engine's 76. A
truth-free pipeline on ten development seeds solved 85.61\% of trials, but only
over the first 82 scored problems in our own likelihood order. No run on
SRBench's official seeds has been completed. Against SymPy, fuller's rule
families prove 33.6\% of 972 SymPy simplifications equal. We report the faults
found on the way and the open work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Symbolic regression (SR) asks for an expression, built from variables,
constants and a set of operators, that predicts a target from data. Genetic
programming~\cite{koza1992gp} and its fixed-length variant, Gene Expression
Programming (GEP)~\cite{ferreira2001gep}, search that space by evolving
expression trees, and physics data sets are a natural test because the
generating law is known~\cite{schmidt2009distilling,udrescu2020aifeynman}.
SRBench~\cite{lacava2021srbench} made that test standard: 133 ground-truth
problems, 119 from the Feynman lectures and 14 Strogatz dynamical systems, with
a symbolic-equivalence check against the true law.

In classical GEP every SR operator takes and returns a real number. The
representation therefore treats $\tanh(\exp(\cos(\log x)))$ as no different in
kind from $m v^2/2$; only the fitness can tell them apart, after the individual
has been built and evaluated. The search is free to grow towers of
transcendentals that fit the training rows to many digits and are not the law.
In our own engine's labelled fits, three quarters of the accurate-but-wrong
models nest three or more transcendentals (the measurement is cited in
\src{phylu src/\allowbreak{}evolve/\allowbreak{}engine.\allowbreak{}rs}, doc comment of \texttt{t\_depth}).

Typed Symbolic Regression (TSR) is Andrew Morgan's answer: make the
transcendental distance between functions part of the type. A subexpression's
type is the largest number of transcendental functions on any path from it to a
leaf. Arithmetic absorbs that depth, a transcendental raises it, and no row
accepts a value that is already two deep. An illegal shape is not penalised;
it has no signature in the symbol table. The core report~\cite{morgan_core}
describes the multi-typed schema, the master symbol table, the geneframe and
the GPU-resident engine; this paper describes the SR and TSR kingdoms that run
on it.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The \textbf{SR kingdom} as built: a real-domain operator set of 26
operators with protected variants that are distinct symbols from the raw
operators, a real-domain evaluator, and a Karva-to-expression converter keyed
on what each operator computes (Section~\ref{sec:kingdom}).
\item The \textbf{TSR kingdom}: transcendental depth carried in three
depth-typed layers, with the measurement over the SRBench laws that fixed the
ceiling at two, and its generated tables for the typed GEP core, including the
joint table with Horton--Strahler stream order
(Section~\ref{sec:tsr}).
\item \textbf{Rewriting inside the search}: fuller's bounded equality-saturation
editors, the constant snap, the physics-prior generator and the canonicaliser
that turns a fitted model into one submitted form
(Section~\ref{sec:editors}).
\item A \textbf{device and host map} of every stage with its source location
(Section~\ref{sec:gpu}).
\item \textbf{Measured results under stated protocols}: three SRBench runs, two
controls, a SymPy-parity measurement, and the faults found and fixed
(Sections~\ref{sec:experiments}--\ref{sec:faults}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{GEP and typed GP.} Ferreira's GEP~\cite{ferreira2001gep} encodes a
tree as a head and a tail read breadth-first, so every string decodes to a
valid tree under one type. Montana's strongly typed GP~\cite{montana1995stgp}
types every argument slot and makes the operators type-aware. The engine here
keeps raw operators and lets the table and the decoder carry the types; the
mechanism is the core report's~\cite{morgan_core}. What is particular to TSR is
the choice of type: not a data type, but an upper bound on transcendental
nesting.

\paragraph{Symbolic regression benchmarks.} SRBench~\cite{lacava2021srbench}
measures the fraction of ten runs per problem that are symbolically exact, at
up to eight hours or $10^6$ evaluations a run. AI Feynman~\cite{udrescu2020aifeynman}
is the strongest published method on the ground-truth track at zero noise:
about 53\% overall~\cite{lacava2021srbench}, which Kartelj and
Djukanovi\'c~\cite{kartelj2023rilsrols} break down as 55.78\% on the Feynman
problems and 27.14\% on the Strogatz systems. Linear scaling of an evolved
expression by a least-squares slope and intercept is due to
Keijzer~\cite{keijzer2003scaling}; the engine applies it to every candidate.

\paragraph{Rewriting.} Equality saturation~\cite{tate2009equality} keeps every
form reachable by a rule set in one e-graph and extracts the best;
egg~\cite{willsey2021egg} made it fast and egglog~\cite{egglog} joined it with
Datalog. fuller uses egglog as a replacement for SymPy~\cite{meurer2017sympy}
in the engine: deterministic, real-domain and bounded.

\paragraph{Selection.} Selection is by hyperspherical fitness
(HFF)~\cite{morgan2026hff}, whose TrueNorth angle and beta-distribution
correction~\cite{cai2013angles} we use as published. Cohort tournaments
reformulate the age-layered population structure~\cite{hornby2006alps}; they
are described in the core report~\cite{morgan_core}. Stream order is the
Horton--Strahler number of a tree~\cite{horton1945streams,strahler1957watershed}.

\section{The SR Kingdom}
\label{sec:kingdom}

\subsection{The operator set and its semantics}

Expressions live in one egglog datatype, \texttt{Math}
(\src{fuller src/\allowbreak{}expr.\allowbreak{}rs}): numeric literals, named variables, and the
operators of Table~\ref{tab:ops}. The engine's alphabet is the
\emph{master semantic set} (\src{src/\allowbreak{}karva.\allowbreak{}rs:59}, \texttt{master\_pset}), 26
operators keyed on what each computes; a test keeps it in lockstep with the
converter. \texttt{diff\_sq}$(a,b)=(a-b)^2$ is a rule over \texttt{Pow2} and
\texttt{Sub}, not a symbol.

The evaluator (\src{src/\allowbreak{}eval.\allowbreak{}rs}) is real-domain only: the square root of a
negative, the logarithm of a non-positive number and division by zero return
NaN, and the caller decides. There is no complex domain, which is why fuller
replaces SymPy in the engine rather than wrapping it. The protected operators
are never mapped to raw ones: they disagree on negatives and zero, and a rewrite
that treated them as equal would be unsound. They are inert under rewriting
except where a rule is sound for the protected form, and domain facts
(\texttt{is-positive}, \texttt{is-nonzero}, \texttt{is-nonneg}) are carried as
relations that guarded rules require (\src{src/\allowbreak{}expr.\allowbreak{}rs},
\texttt{GUARD\_RELATIONS}).

\subsection{Genotype and phenotype}

A chromosome has three genes, each a Karva expression with a head of 34
positions (the run cards of Section~\ref{sec:experiments}), and ten random
numerical constants. \texttt{karva\_to\_terms} (\src{src/\allowbreak{}karva.\allowbreak{}rs:171}) decodes
a gene by the GEP level-order rule into a \texttt{Math} term, ignoring the
non-coding region; \texttt{terms\_to\_karva} re-encodes an edited term into a
gene. Both sides use the semantic id, so the rewriting system never sees an
engine-specific symbol name. In the single-type kingdom every symbol has the one
type \ty{F}; the typed layout reduces to the classical Karva layout, a
property the core report states and the engine's golden test pins~\cite{morgan_core}.

\paragraph{Phenotype.} Each chromosome is scored as up to 15 gene--linker
combinations (subsets of its genes under a linker; \src{phylu
src/\allowbreak{}evolve/\allowbreak{}score.\allowbreak{}wgsl}, header), each under up to three output wrappers, and each
candidate is linearly scaled, $\hat y = a\,f(x)+b$, by least squares on the
training rows~\cite{keijzer2003scaling}. The best candidate by HFF is the
individual's phenotype.

\section{The TSR Kingdom}
\label{sec:tsr}

\subsection{Three depth-typed layers}

TSR gives the same operators types that record transcendental depth:

\begin{itemize}
\item \ty{F}: a plain value --- a variable, a constant, or arithmetic over
plain values;
\item \ty{T1}: the output of one transcendental over \ty{F};
\item \ty{T2}: a transcendental over something already \ty{T1}.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Raising} operators add one layer: \texttt{sin cos tan log exp sqrt abs
tanh asin acos} and the five protected transcendentals, fifteen in all
(\src{fuller src/\allowbreak{}geneframe.\allowbreak{}rs:448}, \texttt{DEPTH\_RAISING}; the engine's list
\src{phylu src/\allowbreak{}evolve/\allowbreak{}engine.\allowbreak{}rs:2271} names the same fifteen, and a
\texttt{pow} counts only when its exponent is not a whole number).
\emph{Level-keeping} unaries (\texttt{neg pow2 pow3 inv}) keep the layer.
Arithmetic is \emph{absorbing}: $+$, $-$, $\times$, $\div$ accept any mix of
layers and yield the highest. A raiser has rows from \ty{F} and from \ty{T1}
only:
\[
\texttt{exp}: \ty{F}\to\ty{T1},\qquad \texttt{exp}: \ty{T1}\to\ty{T2},
\]
and there is no row from \ty{T2} and no \ty{T3}. That missing row is the whole
rule. In the geneframe table (\src{src/\allowbreak{}geneframe.\allowbreak{}rs:471},
\texttt{typed\_depth\_table}) the argument types are many-hot and unordered, so
a binary operator has six rows; in the generated tables for the typed GEP core
(\src{kingdoms/\allowbreak{}tsr/\allowbreak{}depth/\allowbreak{}functions.\allowbreak{}tsv}) argument types are ordered and a
binary operator has nine.

\subsection{Why the ceiling is two}

The ceiling was set from the laws, before any engine work
(\src{kingdoms/\allowbreak{}tsr/\allowbreak{}README.\allowbreak{}md}). Of the 133 SRBench ground-truth models, 128
parse; 78 are at depth 0, 47 at depth 1, 3 at depth 2 and none deeper, and no law
nests two transcendentals directly. The three depth-two laws, such as
$\exp(-(\theta/\sqrt2)^2)$ in which the square root of a constant and the
exponential lie on one path, reach that depth through arithmetic, which is why
arithmetic must absorb rather than reset. All 128 parsed laws type cleanly. Of
eight models the untyped engine had earlier reported, seven did not type (213
to 412 characters); the one that did was 60 characters long and at depth one.

\subsection{Worked examples}

\begin{itemize}
\item $m v^2/2$: every node is \ty{F}.
\item $\tanh(x+e^{y})$: $e^{y}$ is \ty{T1}, the sum absorbs to \ty{T1}, and
$\tanh$ raises to \ty{T2}: legal.
\item $m_0 v/\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}$ (Feynman I.15.10): the square root raises the
\ty{F} argument to \ty{T1}; the quotient absorbs: legal.
\item $\tan(\tan(\log(1/\cos(x_1/x_2))))$: $\cos$ gives \ty{T1}, $\log$ gives
\ty{T2}, and the inner $\tan$ has no row for \ty{T2}: not expressible. This is
the model the untyped engine reported for Feynman I.15.10 at seed 7014 and 180~s,
where TSR recovered the law; it is one seed and is not a kept result
(\src{kingdoms/\allowbreak{}tsr/\allowbreak{}README.\allowbreak{}md}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Legality: two decoders}
\label{sec:legal}

Two versions of TSR exist in the code, and the results of
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} come from the first.

\paragraph{Depth derived at decode.} In the engine that produced the SRBench
results, the layout is the single-type Karva layout and the device decoder
derives a layer for every node bottom-up (\src{phylu
src/\allowbreak{}evolve/\allowbreak{}typed\_\allowbreak{}decode.\allowbreak{}wgsl:229-235}: a raiser over \ty{T2} returns no type).
A gene with no derivation does not express: its program has length zero, and it
is counted. In the CASCADE2 study (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}) 31.0 million
genes were not expressed over the 133 fits (sum of the \texttt{TYPED refused}
counts in \src{phylu logs/\allowbreak{}CASCADE2/\allowbreak{}p1/\allowbreak{}*/\allowbreak{}run.\allowbreak{}log}); on Strogatz shear flow~2,
476{,}641 of about 6.65 million decoded genes (1{,}108 generations of 2{,}000
three-gene individuals), about 7\%. A gene that does not express does not kill
its chromosome: a three-gene chromosome scores on its other genes and the gene
rides along unscored, so the final population still holds genes past the
ceiling (\src{kingdoms/\allowbreak{}tsr/\allowbreak{}README.\allowbreak{}md}).

\paragraph{Depth in the layout.} For the typed GEP core, a generator
(\src{fuller kingdoms/\allowbreak{}tsr/\allowbreak{}gen\_\allowbreak{}tables.\allowbreak{}py}) writes the depth limit, and
optionally the Horton--Strahler stream-order limit, as types: each node's type
is an upper bound on the subtree under its slot, every operator has one row per
legal combination of argument types, and a separate \ty{ROOT} type has a twin of
every row (\src{kingdoms/\allowbreak{}tsr/\allowbreak{}depth/\allowbreak{}TYPES.\allowbreak{}md}). A gene in these layouts is legal
by construction: variation draws each cell from the domain of its type, so no
individual is built and then discarded. A checker
(\src{kingdoms/\allowbreak{}tsr/\allowbreak{}check\_\allowbreak{}types.\allowbreak{}py}) compared the order and depth tables with
the engine's own order and depth code on 4{,}335{,}881 exhaustively enumerated
trees of up to nine nodes and 300{,}000 random trees, with no disagreement
(\src{fuller docs/\allowbreak{}PLAN\_\allowbreak{}tsr\_\allowbreak{}typed\_\allowbreak{}gep.\allowbreak{}md}, findings). Table~\ref{tab:variants}
gives the sizes. Edited trees are re-typed by keeping each node's row while it
still fits the heads, because typing a rewritten tree exactly from scratch
failed 72\% of identity rewrites for want of head capacity (same file, finding
(b)).

\paragraph{What a type cannot carry.} Every type here is an upper bound. A
lower bound --- the laws never apply $\exp$ to a bare variable (10 of 10 uses
take an argument of order two, \src{phylu src/\allowbreak{}evolve/\allowbreak{}order.\allowbreak{}rs:20-27}) --- cannot
be a type, because a bare variable fits every upper bound.

\section{Fitness Evaluation}
\label{sec:fitness}

\paragraph{Objectives.} For each candidate the device reduces mean squared
error (capped at the target's variance), $1-R^2$ (capped at 1) and mean
absolute deviation (capped at the target's MAD), each on the training and on
the validation rows: six objectives
(\src{phylu src/\allowbreak{}evolve/\allowbreak{}engine.\allowbreak{}rs:2349}, \texttt{hff\_columns};
\texttt{Caps::objectives}, \src{:2631}). Validation is an objective, not a
filter, and there is no parsimony objective; the fixed head bounds size.

\paragraph{HFF and TrueNorth.} The objective vector $\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^m$ is
projected by the hff library's TrueNorth construction
(\src{hff src/\allowbreak{}core\_\allowbreak{}functions.\allowbreak{}rs:104}): with energy $E=\sum x_i^2$ and energy
score $e=1-\min(E/m,1)$, the vector is lifted into $\mathbb{R}^{m+1}$ and its
angle from the pole $(0,\dots,0,1)$ is $\theta=\arccos e$. The engine calls the
library with normalisation off (\src{phylu src/\allowbreak{}evolve/\allowbreak{}engine.\allowbreak{}rs:2585}).
Tournaments select on $\theta$, lower preferred, within the cohort rule of the
core report~\cite{morgan_core}.

\paragraph{The law bar.} The early stop uses $\log_{10} p$, where
$p=I_{\sin^2\theta}\big(\tfrac{m-1}{2},\tfrac12\big)$ is the probability that a
uniformly random direction lies within $\theta$ of the pole
(\src{hff src/\allowbreak{}higd.\allowbreak{}rs:126}; \src{engine.\allowbreak{}rs:2259}). A fit stops when
$\log_{10}p\le-19$ and $1-R^2\le10^{-10}$ on both the training and the
validation rows (\src{engine.\allowbreak{}rs:8852-8864}). The training side was added after
Strogatz bacres2 stopped with validation $1-R^2=9.46\times10^{-11}$ and
training $1.03\times10^{-9}$, on a function that was not the law; over 117
earlier early stops, all 113 real wins had training $1-R^2\le10^{-10}$ (code
comment at the same lines). Some runs below used $10^{-6}$; each says so.

\paragraph{Cases.} The fitness cases are the rows of an SRBench data set: up to
5{,}000 rows in the engine's stratified order, split 80/20 into training and
validation (4{,}000 and 1{,}000 for the Feynman sets), at noise level zero.

\section{Rewriting Inside the Search}
\label{sec:editors}

fuller proposes; the tournament disposes. Each editor takes individuals from the
population, edits them as \texttt{Math} terms, re-encodes them into genes and
returns them for selection, with the original kept.

\paragraph{Rule families.} fuller's rules are grouped in named egglog rulesets
(\src{fuller src/\allowbreak{}ruleset/\allowbreak{}}): algebraic identities, powers, sign,
distribution, rational forms, trigonometry, cofunctions, collection, a wide
family of commutativity and associativity for the equality check, rules mined
from SymPy's own power and trigonometric code, and a sharing family. The
families are not mutually confluent: distribution saturated with trigonometry or
with rational forms grows the e-graph without bound, so every saturation is
bounded and families are kept apart.

\paragraph{Denoise.} \texttt{denoise} (\src{fuller src/\allowbreak{}extract.\allowbreak{}rs:408})
saturates a term under algebra, powers and sign for at most 40 iterations,
extracts the $k$ cheapest equivalent forms, evaluates each on the training rows
and returns the smallest whose $R^2$ loss against the input is within a
tolerance; otherwise the input, unchanged. It never raises.

\paragraph{Snap.} A fitted constant within a relative tolerance of a known value
($\pi$, $e$, $\sqrt2$, $\tau$, $\tfrac12$, $\tfrac13$, $\tfrac14$, $G$, $c$,
$k_e$; \src{fuller src/\allowbreak{}snap.\allowbreak{}rs:26}) is replaced by that value, and the symbol is
recorded for rendering; the expression stays numeric. Problem-specific
combinations are deliberately not in the library. In the engine the snap
candidates are grafted and checked on the device (\src{fuller
src/\allowbreak{}lint/\allowbreak{}snap\_\allowbreak{}graft.\allowbreak{}wgsl:88}, \src{snap\_\allowbreak{}guard.\allowbreak{}wgsl:136}).

\paragraph{Typed snap and fold.} Typed snap matches subtrees of promising genes
to a library of typed shapes; fold replaces a subtree that is near constant on
the data by its mean, with the statistics computed in one device pass
(\src{phylu src/\allowbreak{}evolve/\allowbreak{}gpu\_\allowbreak{}fold.\allowbreak{}rs:49}); the ratio fold replaces a subtree that
is a constant multiple of its own descendant on the data.

\paragraph{Physics prior.} \texttt{physics::generate} (\src{fuller
src/\allowbreak{}physics.\allowbreak{}rs}) is a one-to-many generator of physics-shaped edits (squared
differences, axis-aligned coordinate pairs, inverse powers), with no evaluation
inside it; edits that build new structure are tagged speculative so the caller
can check them on held-back rows.

\paragraph{The canonicaliser.} After a fit, the reported model goes through the
kitchen sink (\src{phylu examples/\allowbreak{}kitchen\_\allowbreak{}sink.\allowbreak{}rs}), which runs every editor
above on it in three rounds, ranks the pool by HFF on the device
(\src{src/\allowbreak{}evolve/\allowbreak{}hff\_\allowbreak{}gpu.\allowbreak{}rs:257}), re-scores the finalists in \texttt{f64},
spells each one plainly, rationalised and in SRBench's algebra, and keeps only
spellings whose predictions survive SRBench's rounding of floats on the
training rows. One form is emitted. In the truth-free protocol it reads only the
training partition.

earlier runs, so this is history, not a typing effect.

\paragraph{Control: the stream-order rule as a hard constraint.} A rule bolted
on after decoding --- root stream order at most three, argument windows, and a
table of permitted unary stacks --- was run on 83 laws at 90~s under the TF\_DEV1
protocol. It solved 84.69\% (81 scored problems) against TF\_DEV1's 85.61\% (82);
on the 81 common laws, 686 against 700 solved trials of 810, 16 laws better and
13 worse; its largest losses were Lorentz and Gaussian laws, for example Feynman
II.13.23, 1 of 10 against 8 (\src{logs/\allowbreak{}AB\_\allowbreak{}ORDER\_\allowbreak{}EASY/\allowbreak{}summary.\allowbreak{}txt};
\src{fuller docs/\allowbreak{}PLAN\_\allowbreak{}tsr\_\allowbreak{}typed\_\allowbreak{}gep.\allowbreak{}md} \S1.2). It is off in every other run.

\paragraph{Control: the rebuild onto the typed core.} On the first 40 laws, ten
development seeds and $10^6$ evaluations, the single-type kingdom rebuilt on the
typed GEP core passed the engine's law bar in 380 of 400 seed-runs against the
old engine's 382; 33 laws gave the same count, four went up (+5) and three down
($-7$) (\src{phylu docs/\allowbreak{}reports/\allowbreak{}2026-10-20\_\allowbreak{}sr\_\allowbreak{}typed\_\allowbreak{}gep.\allowbreak{}md}). The two arms do
not draw the same random numbers.

\paragraph{Against AI Feynman.} AI Feynman finds an exact solution in about
53\% of runs at zero noise~\cite{lacava2021srbench}, 55.78\% on the Feynman
problems and 27.14\% on the Strogatz systems~\cite{kartelj2023rilsrols}, with up
to eight hours a run and no access to the answer. Our CASCADE2 and AB1 counts
are of the same order but come from one seed, a 90~s budget, and the answer key
inside the loop; our truth-free rate covers the easiest 82 problems. None of
these is comparable with the published figure. The comparison that would count
--- the pre-registered official seeds under the truth-free protocol, over all
130 scored problems --- has not been run.

\section{Faults Found and Fixed}
\label{sec:faults}

These are part of the method: each was found by a measurement that disagreed
with an expectation, and each is fixed in the commit named.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The answer key was inside the loop.} Every SRBench count before
2026-09-30 stopped on SRBench's verdict and chose the submitted spelling by
it; an external review also found test rows reaching the finisher, an
inheritable environment variable that carried the truth data set, and a
pool-relative HFF normalisation (\src{phylu
docs/\allowbreak{}PLAN\_\allowbreak{}srbench\_\allowbreak{}competition.\allowbreak{}md} v2, findings C1--C3). The truth-free
pipeline answers each; CASCADE2 and AB1 remain development results, and PROD1,
begun on the official seeds with the key in the loop, was stopped and is not
reported.
\item \textbf{The HFF kernel had four defects its parity tests could not see.}
The tests re-implemented the kernel's arithmetic in Rust. A test that dispatches
the kernel and compares it with the host's own candidate walk found that NaN
candidates won the argmin (Metal folds \texttt{a != a} to false), that a tower
term divided by 6 where the host divided by 4, and two more (fuller
\texttt{bbd4d77a}).
\item \textbf{The scorer's compensated sums were optimised away on the
device.} On $y=2x$ over 40{,}000 rows the device gave $a=1.9999967$ and
$1-R^2=10^{-9}$; every intermediate is now kept and division and square root are
corrected by an exact residual, bit-identical with a CPU twin (fuller
\texttt{e2fc1738}).
\item \textbf{TSR genes were 2{,}743~ms a generation} because the evaluator
gave every thread scratch for the layout's worst case, zero-filled; length
classes cut it to 633~ms (fuller \texttt{35b88fb8}), and the later fixes of
Figure~\ref{fig:speed} to 127.5~ms.
\item \textbf{Fold read the wrong statistics.} From phylu \texttt{5ba2ff53}
(2026-10-20) to \texttt{f706393d} the fold read subtree statistics with a
stride of 128 from an array whose stride was the evaluator's bound, so most
statistics belonged to another expression: on Feynman I.6.2a, TSR arm C folded
22 rows instead of 293. Every SR and TSR run in that window ran with a nearly
inactive fold (\src{fuller docs/\allowbreak{}PLAN\_\allowbreak{}outstanding\_\allowbreak{}2026-10-21.\allowbreak{}md}, T57). A test
(phylu \texttt{459d2214}) plants a blob in 40 rows of one beat: with the old
stride the fold finds 4 of 40 and 276 of 292 subtrees are non-finite; today it
finds 59, with 1 of 318 non-finite. Before \texttt{5ba2ff53} the evaluator's
bound was the fold's stride of 128, so runs before it were not affected.
\item \textbf{The beam put the live population back only when it kept a
survivor}, after scoring its mutants in a scratch population over the device's
(\src{tsr\_\allowbreak{}speed.\allowbreak{}md}, ``Found on the way'').
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The measurement that set the TSR ceiling is clean: the laws never exceed two
transcendentals on a path, and most reported towers do. What the measurements do
not yet show is that removing those shapes helps the search find laws. The one
same-engine comparison, AB1, is one seed; there the untyped engine certified
four more laws and the difference is within chance. A ceiling on stream order,
bolted on after decoding, lost 14 solved trials of 810, mostly on laws whose
true form the rule's table had been built from tidied spellings that the raw
genes did not use. The lesson we draw is about where a constraint sits rather
than whether it is right: a rule applied after decoding throws away individuals
the variation operators have already spent effort on, and a rule built from
tidied spellings rejects raw spellings of the same law. The typed-core TSR
tables address both --- the rule is the table, and the table is checked against
the engine's own order and depth code --- at the price of genes up to 208 times
wider. The speed work of Figure~\ref{fig:speed} brought arm C within a factor
of 2.8 of the single type per generation; whether it pays in laws found is what
the arms of the plan will measure.

The clearest evidence that the two systems do more together than apart is the
step from 74 to 79 in CASCADE2: rewriting a near miss into the law was cheaper
than evolving it, for five laws. That step used the answer key to choose among
spellings; in TF\_DEV1, where the canonicaliser chooses alone, the rate counting
only its own emitted forms is 83.66\%, and the engine's plain form, used when
the canonicaliser found no spelling that survives SRBench's rounding, accounts
for the rest of the 85.61\%.

\section{Limitations and Open Work}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No reported run on SRBench's official seeds.} The
pre-registered run (the first ten official seeds, the truth-free protocol, all
130 scored problems, TSR and untyped paired, \src{fuller
docs/\allowbreak{}DESIGN\_\allowbreak{}tsr\_\allowbreak{}vs\_\allowbreak{}sr\_\allowbreak{}srbench\_\allowbreak{}runpod.\allowbreak{}md}) has not been run. Two official
seeds were watched during judge-side fixes, which a report must state.
\item \textbf{Runs with a nearly inactive fold.} SR\_OLD\_NEW (report of
2026-10-20), the TSR speed measurements (2026-10-20) and the Feynman I.6.2a
learning check ran between phylu \texttt{5ba2ff53} (2026-10-20) and the fold's
fix \texttt{f706393d} (2026-10-21), so their fold read another expression's
statistics. CASCADE2 (2026-09-30), TF\_DEV1 (2026-10-02), AB1 (2026-10-08) and
PROD1 (2026-09-30) predate \texttt{f706393d} but also \texttt{5ba2ff53}, when
the stride and the evaluator's bound were both 128.
\item \textbf{The truth-free rate is on the easiest problems.} TF\_DEV1 covers
the first 82 scored problems of an order built to put easy laws first.
\item \textbf{One seed for the typing comparison}, and in AB1 the kitchen sink
chose among up to 30 spellings with the answer key.
\item \textbf{The typed-core TSR arms have not run.} The tables, the checker,
the re-typing and the speed fixes exist; the experiment of
\src{PLAN\_\allowbreak{}tsr\_\allowbreak{}typed\_\allowbreak{}gep.\allowbreak{}md} \S7 (arms A--F, paired by law and seed, McNemar
counts and a law bootstrap) has not been run, and nothing here says whether
depth or order in the layout changes recovery. A three-seed learning check on
Feynman I.6.2a (arm C found the law on 3 of 3 seeds with the mutation rate
applied per type, 2 of 3 without) ran in the fold-fault window and is not a
result.
\item \textbf{A gene that does not express still occupies a chromosome} in the
decode-time version, so typing there adds unscored genes rather than removing
shapes.
\item \textbf{Parity is stale.} The full measurement is from May 2026; rule
families have been added since and the simplify corpus did not complete in the
September re-run.
\item \textbf{Lower bounds are not types.} ``$\exp$ never takes a bare
variable'' holds for every law and cannot be written as an upper-bound type.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

TSR makes transcendental depth a type: three depth-typed layers, absorbing
arithmetic, raising transcendentals, and no row past two. The ceiling is the
one the 133 SRBench laws support, and in the typed GEP core it is part of the
layout, so every individual is legal by construction. On the engine as run, TSR
with fuller's editors recovered 74 laws, and 79 with post-search rewriting, on
one development seed at 90~s with the answer key as the stop; it solved
85.61\% of truth-free trials on the 82 easiest scored problems; and in the one
same-engine control it certified 72 laws against the untyped engine's 76, a
difference within chance. fuller proves 33.6\% of SymPy's simplifications on
its own rules. The official-seed run under the truth-free protocol, and the
typed-core arms, are the open work.
