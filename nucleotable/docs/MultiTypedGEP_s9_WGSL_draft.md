# 9. The WGSL kingdom's symbol table (built one row at a time)

The kingdom holds any WGSL compute kernel over a declared **interface**: its storage bindings (each an input, read-only, or an output, read-write, with an element type `<e>` and a shape over the uniform's fields), its uniform block's fields, and the target's limits. Rows that mention a binding or a field are **families**, generated once per declared binding or field; the Gram interface (`phi: array<f32>` in, `h: array<f32>` out with shape `cfg.r_rows x cfg.d`, `cfg { d, m, r_rows, r_off }`) is one worked instance, never the default. Notation: `<b>` an output binding, `<i>` an input binding, `<f>` a uniform field, `<e>` the binding's element type (`f32`, `u32`, `i32`, `vec4<f32>`, ...). A 2-D output declares `shape <b> <R_b> <C_b>` (rows and columns, each an expression over fields) and is laid out row-major with stride `C_b`; a 1-D output declares `shape <b> <L_b>`. Every binding with load or store rows declares a length lower bound of at least one. `KNOB_BROW`, `KNOB_BCOL`, `KNOB_WROW`, `KNOB_WCOL` are `const`s the entry point writes from its grid node. `lit_u32`, `lit_f32`, `lit_bool`, `lit_i32` are identity helpers that keep a literal out of constant evaluation where a slot needs it (batch 7, "Bare and runtime literals": elsewhere a literal is spelled bare).

Batch 1 (2026-10-21): the kernel skeleton, the epilogue's store, the ownership rows. Batch 2: the remaining builtins and axes, u32 and f32 arithmetic, comparisons and logic, input loads and uniform fields. Batch 3: registers and private arrays, fuel strata, loops and counters, uniform provenance (fields, workgroup ids, uniform counters, `workgroupUniformLoad`), the barrier. Batch 4: the W phase and workgroup-array writes, the stratum map's arrays and reads, reductions, atomics, subgroups. Batch 5: vectors and their arrays, i32 and the remaining scalar families, the strided, grouped and lane partitions, arenas and the relaxed conversions, the storage barrier. Batch 6: f16, matrices, the tile-counter offsets of the strided and grouped partitions, the mirror store, knob references, the grid variants, the vec2/vec3 and i32 workgroup arrays, i32 atomics. The table is complete with batch 6; the families deferred are listed at the end.

## Types (growing)

**Legality and determinism.** The kingdom's legality is: no undefined behaviour, no race, uniform control where WGSL demands it, bounded work, every access in range, the epilogue's store done. WGSL's floating-point edge results (`x / 0`, `sqrt(-1)`, overflow, `smoothstep` with equal edges, a singular `inverse`, f16 overflow, `u32(NaN)`) are WGSL-defined values (IEEE special values or an indeterminate value of the type), not undefined behaviour: those rows stay and fitness scores whatever they compute. `determinism strict` means memory-order determinism (no race, no consumed atomic order); it claims nothing about floating-point edge determinism (ruled, Codex 4).

## Batch 3: registers, private arrays, fuel strata, loops and counters, uniform provenance, the barrier

**Fuel strata, applied to every statement type.** From this batch every statement and block type carries a fuel stratum `_f<k>`, `k` in `0..=4`: `stmt.L0_f2`, `block.C1_f0`, `block.O0_f4`, ... The meaning of stratum `k`: one execution of the statement performs at most `H * 2^(6k)` node executions, `H` the layout's total head length (the walk reads each head row at most once, so a tree has at most `H` functions). Every row of batches 1 and 2 that takes or gives a statement or block type now exists once per stratum with **all** its statement inputs and its output at one stratum (`block.seq2_L0_f2: (stmt.L0_f2, stmt.L0_f2) -> block.L0_f2`; `stmt.if_L0_f1: (bool.flag, block.C0_f1) -> stmt.L0_f1`; `store.<b>_O0_f3 -> stmt.O0_f3`; `own.stmt_L0_f0`, ... at every stratum), and the root takes the interface's budget `K`: `kernel.entry_safe: (grid.grid, block.L0_f<K>, block.O0_f<K>)`. Only loop rows raise the stratum (by their trip class); the lift rows below raise it without text. So a kernel of budget `K` executes at most `H * 2^(6K)` nodes an invocation and terminates: the hang (A1 P3, H10) has no tree.

**Rung ladder.** A loop at rung `<X><d>` has a body at `<X><d+1>` and binds the counter of depth `d+1`; depths run `0..=5`, so five loops nest and no row takes a `block.*6`. Types added: `stmt.L<d>`, `block.L<d>`, `stmt.C<d>`, `block.C<d>` for `d` in `1..=5`; `stmt.O<d>`, `block.O<d>` for `d` in `1..=4`; `block.B<d>` for `d` in `1..=5` (a loop body that breaks: conditional statements, the guard, conditional statements; no barrier, so an invocation that leaves early skips no barrier).

## Batch 4: the W phase and workgroup memory, reductions, atomics, subgroups

**The stratum map.** The interface's workgroup-memory map lists, per element type, the power-of-two strata it has; each listed `(e, 2^j)` is one array `wg_<e>_<j>`, declared in the prologue; their total is within the target. The worked map here: `f32` at `2^11`, `u32` at `2^10` (batch 3), `atomic<u32>` at `2^8`. `KNOB_THREADS = X * Y` is a prologue constant.

**The W value family (form A, ruled batch 4).** Outside W a foreign workgroup read is an ordinary value (`wg.get_f_11 -> f32.real`), so a seed's `x = wg[i] + 1.0` reads exactly. **The tiled dispatch (ST9 phase 2, 2026-10-22).** An image interface declares `tiled_dispatch`: its prologue binds `@group(3) @binding(0) var<uniform> dispatch_tile: DispatchTile` (`origin`, `grid`: the submit's first workgroup and the whole grid, in workgroups), and `kernel.entry_safe` takes the builtins of the submit as `gid_tile`, `wid_tile` and binds `let wid = wid_tile + dispatch_tile.origin.xyz; let gid = gid_tile + origin * (WG_X, WG_Y, 1); let nwg = dispatch_tile.grid.xyz;` before the declarations, so `gid`, `wid`, `nwg` mean the whole dispatch in every row. The prologue also declares the pipeline-overridable constant `override DISPATCH_TILED: bool = true;` (the interface's, never a row's) and the lets are `select(<id>_tile, <id>_tile + offset, DISPATCH_TILED)` (`nwg` from `nwg_tile`, the submit's `num_workgroups`): the host creates the pipeline with `DISPATCH_TILED = false` for a pass of one submit, so the ids are the builtins and Apple's compiler folds the tile uniform away (2026-10-22, measured 2.9 % of volumepassage at 64 x 64), and with `true` for several submits. The host (`fuller::wgsl::image`) splits a dispatch into submits of whole workgroups and writes each submit's tile. The split is the work guard's: the expressed tree's own bound (`TrueKingdom::tree_work_bound`, the sum over its nodes of the product of the trip caps of the loops enclosing each: a tile row's literal `n`, else `2^(6c)` for the stratum class `c` the loop row raises; an argument spelled before the loop text is outside it) times the invocations of a submit is at most the per-submit cap. The encoded volumepassage is bounded by its 50 x 7 loops, far below `H * 2^(6K)`; a tree whose single workgroup is over the cap cannot be split into whole workgroups and is not submitted.

**Bare and runtime literals (2026-10-22).** A literal is spelled bare (`3.0`, `50u`) unless a slot needs it kept out of constant evaluation. The rule, from WGSL: a const-expression is a literal, a `const`, or an operator or const built-in whose every operand is one (8.1.1); a `lit_*` call, a `let`, a `var`, a builtin parameter and a binding are not. A const-expression that fails is a shader-creation error: a float const-expression that overflows or is NaN or infinite (15.7.3), an integer overflow of `+ - *` or `<<` and `MIN / -1` when both operands are constant (8.8, 8.10); and, with **one** constant operand, an integer `/` or `%` by a constant zero (8.8) and a shift by a constant count at or past the width (8.10). Mixed-operand rules of the other built-ins (`smoothstep` edges, `clamp` bounds, `extractBits`/`insertBits` offset and count) need every named operand constant. So:
- Every value type of the families (`f32.real`, `u32.index`, `vec3<f32>.real`, the matrices, their `w` twins) is **never a const-expression**: its literal is the **runtime literal** `literal.<fam>` spelled `lit_*({v})`, and every other producer reads a runtime name (checked over the generated table: every row giving a value type from no value argument reads a register, array, counter, builtin, binding, uniform, helper or runtime literal).
- Each scalar family has a **bare literal** type (`f32.bare`, `f16.bare`, `u32.bare_index`, `u32.bare_count`, `i32.bare`, `bool.bare`), produced only by `bare.<fam>` spelled `{v}` (`{v}u`, `{v}h`) over the family's `C_T`, and consumed only by the twins `<row>_bare<i>` that `lib.finish_bare` makes: slot `i` of a statement row (a statement is not an expression; the literal alone cannot fail), or of an expression row with another argument of a value type (that argument is not constant, so neither is the row's expression), except an integer divisor or shift count (the one-constant-operand errors above). One bare slot a twin.
- The literal-only types whose every consumer is safe constant are spelled bare outright: `u32.lit64` (a loop bound's assignment, `min(.., KNOB_B* - 1u)`, `k * KNOB_THREADS <= 64 * 1024`), `u32.lit4096` (a stride `let`), `u32.litpow2_<j>`, `f32.nonneg`, `i32.nonneg`, the loop caps `min(x, 2^c u)` and the one-trip bounds `1u`. `uniform.lit` keeps `lit_u32({0})`: uniform arithmetic of literals could overflow. `u32.payload` (its knob product can pass `2^32`), bits, hash, codes, fixed point and sign literals stay runtime literals.

The reader takes a runtime literal in slot `s` of row `r` as the bare literal in `r_bare<s>` wherever the table has that twin, the first such slot of the node. **The counted loop's spelling (2026-10-22).** `n = {bound}; kc = 0u; loop { k = kc; if (k >= n) { break; } kc = k + step; {body} }` (`kxc`/`kx`/`nx` for the divergent loops): no continuing block, so naga writes no `loop_init` (the form Apple's compiler hangs on, docs/reports/2026-10-21_metal_hang_k2); `k` is the trip inside the body, n after the last trip, the trip of a `break` after it, and a `continue` takes the step. The earlier spelling (`k = 4294967295u; loop { k = k + 1u; if (k >= n) { break; } ... }`) cost volumepassage 4.3 % at 64 x 64 against this one; this one terminates on the hang reproducer.

The 1,000-gene loop on Gram, `img_volumepassage_k1` and `img_volumepassage` is the check that no tree of the table is refused at shader creation.

**Declarations are row data (2026-10-22).** The entry point's `<declarations>` are not the whole register file: every row whose spelling names a kernel-scope variable (`reg.<r><n>`, `parr.<p><n>`, `ureg.U<n>`, `safe.tile_counter<d>`, `safe.xtile_counter<d>`, each loop row's `n<d>`/`k<d>` or `nx<d>`/`kx<d>`) carries that variable's declaration as its `declares` column of `functions.tsv`, and the renderer emits the fragments of the rows in the expressed tree, deduplicated, in the order of `declarations.wgsl`. Declarations stay zero-initialised as before, so every value is unchanged.

Constants: an interface may append its own literals to the `C_T` of `f32.real`, `i32.int`, `u32.index`, `u32.count` (`extra_constants`, spelled as the shader spells them). `C_T` is append-only, so an interface with none generates exactly its old table. Fuel: the interface carries its own budget K (`fuel_budget`); a counted loop inside a counted loop needs K = 2.

**Reading a cuneus shader (the reader rule).** A seed's helpers are inlined. A shader reads exactly when inlining gives the same tree: a helper with no early return and no side effect on anything but its result, whose local `let`s are registers (a value used twice is stored once in a register, never copied), so the tree is the original's computation operation for operation. Spellings that differ from the source without changing any lane's arithmetic: a vector plus or divided by a scalar is the vector op over `shape.splat`; `if (gid >= dims) { return; }` at the top of the entry is the store's own bounds guard. A shader that cannot be read this way is outside the kingdom with its reason, never rewritten. Outside the kingdom today: every shader with a `texture_2d` or sampler binding (reads of images: rorschach, tree, galaxy, kuwahara, fluid, jfa, tameimp, mandelbulb, orbits, lich, physarum, pinscreen, pathtracing, fft, debugscreen, blackhole, meshlab_post) and every shader with a read-write storage buffer the kernel writes (synth, veridisquo, blockgame, bugberman, cnn, buddhabrot, galaxy, gaussian) until those families are added; multi-pass shaders read only one pass at a time, with the other passes' outputs as inputs (textures), so they wait for the texture-read family.

## Batch 9: workgroup memory as two pools of aligned regions, and the three phases (UI1, 2026-10-22)

Generated only for an interface that declares `wg_pool` (`kingdoms/wgsl_true/pool.py`); it replaces the stratum map of batch 4 there. A seed declares any number of workgroup arrays; the kingdom declares two, `wg_pool: array<u32, 6080>` (every non-atomic array: u32, f32 and i32 bit-cast, a `vec4<f32>` array as four lane regions) and `wg_apool: array<atomic<u32>, 2112>` (every atomic array): 32,768 bytes, the target's workgroup memory, so no gene exceeds it whatever it touches. Each seed array is a **region** at a base the reader allocates.

**Aligned regions keep every write phase race-free.** A plain region's base is `m * KNOB_THREADS` (`u32.wgbase` over the bare block count `u32.wgblk`); an own element is `lidx + k * T` (`u32.wgown`, any per-invocation `k`). Two own writes `b1 + t1 + k1 T` and `b2 + t2 + k2 T` of one phase meet only if `t1 = t2 (mod T)`, that is the same invocation. A write is guarded by the pool's length (`if (o < 6080u)`), never clamped (a clamp collapses lanes); the sum wraps modulo 2^32, which T (a power of two) divides, so the rule holds through the wrap. A `vec4<f32>` array is four lane regions (structure of arrays), each its own aligned region. An array the seed writes block by block (`t * C + j`, `j < C`) is stored transposed (`j * T + t`), a bijection the reader applies to every access of that array, so its writes are own writes too. Reads are clamped (`min(b + i, 6079u)`).

## Batch 9: workgroup memory as two pools of aligned regions, and the three phases (UI1, 2026-10-22)

Generated only for an interface that declares `wg_pool` (`kingdoms/wgsl_true/pool.py`); it replaces the stratum map of batch 4 there. A seed declares any number of workgroup arrays; the kingdom declares two, `wg_pool: array<u32, 6080>` (every non-atomic array: u32, f32 and i32 bit-cast, a `vec4<f32>` array as four lane regions) and `wg_apool: array<atomic<u32>, 2112>` (every atomic array): 32,768 bytes, the target's workgroup memory, so no gene exceeds it whatever it touches. Each seed array is a **region** at a base the reader allocates.

**Aligned regions keep every write phase race-free.** A plain region's base is `m * KNOB_THREADS` (`u32.wgbase` over the bare block count `u32.wgblk`); an own element is `lidx + k * T` (`u32.wgown`, any per-invocation `k`). Two own writes `b1 + t1 + k1 T` and `b2 + t2 + k2 T` of one phase meet only if `t1 = t2 (mod T)`, that is the same invocation. A write is guarded by the pool's length (`if (o < 6080u)`), never clamped (a clamp collapses lanes); the sum wraps modulo 2^32, which T (a power of two) divides, so the rule holds through the wrap. A `vec4<f32>` array is four lane regions (structure of arrays), each its own aligned region. An array the seed writes block by block (`t * C + j`, `j < C`) is stored transposed (`j * T + t`), a bijection the reader applies to every access of that array, so its writes are own writes too. Reads are clamped (`min(b + i, 6079u)`).

## Batch 10: the UI1 gaps (2026-10-23)

Each row family below closes a gap where a V24 seed was legal under its contract but no row or type of the table could prove it. Each is generated only for a module interface, so the Gram and image tables stay byte-identical.

- **Pack and unpack in a phase.** `bits.pack*` and `bits.unpack*` get `w` twins over `w` inputs. They are pure functions of their argument and read no workgroup element, so no foreign read can enter a phase through them (seeds5b seed23).
- **Atomics of the pool in a phase, read as values.** `atomic.wg_load_w` and `atomic.wg_<fun>_w` already existed in the table. The reader had matched only the plain rows, so a phase's `atomicLoad(&wg[t])` used as a position (`index.of_atomicold_w`) did not read (seeds5b seed01, seed09). This was a reader fix: no row was added.
- **The swap-pair phase (E rung): sort and butterfly steps.** `wg.xchg_L<d>_f<k>: (u32.uniform h, u32.xmode m, u32.xspan xs, block.E, block.L)` is spelled `workgroupBarrier(); { let xh0 = {0}; let xh = xh0 & (0u - xh0); let xm = {1}; let xs = {2}; {3} } workgroupBarrier(); {4}`. `xh` is the lowest set bit of `h`: a power of two, or 0. Inside the phase an invocation's pair in block `b` is `(b * xs + i, b * xs + (i | xh))`, with `i = xidx(p, xh, xm)` built from its own element `p` (`u32.wgown`). In mode 0, `i = p`, and the pair is taken only when bit `xh` of `p` is clear. In mode 1, `i` is `p` with a zero bit inserted at `xh` (`p` below 2^31), so that bit of `i` is always clear. The E rung writes workgroup memory only through `wg.xset_<lo|hi>_<e>_E: (u32.xblk b, u32.wgown p, w value)` and reads it only through `wg.xload_<lo|hi>_<e>_E: (register, u32.xblk, u32.wgown)`. A load reads 0 when the pair is not taken. Every access is guarded by `xok(p, xh, xm) && i < xs` in the row's own text. Both maps from `p` to `i` are injective across invocations, and every `i` they take has bit `xh` clear. So within one block no element belongs to two pairs, and two blocks never share an element. No element is therefore read and written by two invocations between the phase's barriers, whatever blocks, `p` or `h` a gene names. Every other family of the W rung exists at E over the `w` values, including loops. The rung has no own writes and no foreign reads.
  The reader takes a segment as a swap-pair phase when every write of its swapped arrays is the low or the high element of one `h` and one mode. The high element may be spelled `lo ^ h`, `lo + h` or `lo | h`; these are equal when bit `h` of `lo` is clear. In mode 0, a condition `(lo ^ h) > lo` or `(lo & h) == 0u` must stand around every access. The insert forms read are `((p >> s) << (s + 1u)) | (p & (h - 1u))` and `(p / h) * 2u * h + (p % h)`. `h` must be provably a power of two, and in mode 1 nonzero: `1u << s`, a power-of-two literal, or `(1u << a) >> (j + 1u)` with `j` a counter below `a`. Each swapped array is placed at a multiple of its span, the smallest power of two at least its length.
- **`diff` in the safety contract.** `diff cfg.arrived_end cfg.arrived_begin 0 hi` states the run program's difference of two fields, never negative, as a contract fact. The reader's bound of `a - b` now holds only where the subtraction cannot wrap: by the contract's `diff`, by `min a >= max b`, or for a counter below its own bound `n` subtracted from `n` or `n - 1`. Before this change, a loop bounded by `a - b` took `a`'s bound even when `a - b` could wrap.
- **The block-write phase (Y rung): arrays filled block by block and used otherwise.** `wg.bphase_L<d>_f<k>: (u32.bcount C, block.Y, block.L)` is spelled `workgroupBarrier(); { let bc = {0}; {1} } workgroupBarrier(); {2}`. At Y, `wg.bset_<e>_Y: (u32.bblk b, u32.indexw k, u32.indexw j, w value)` writes word `(b + k) * (T * bc) + lidx * bc + min(j, bc - 1u)`, guarded by `k < 4096u` and the pool's length; `wg.bload_<e>_Y` reads the same word into a register. Distinct `(group, invocation, j)` name distinct words: `lidx * bc + j` is below `T * bc`, and the group term is below 2^31, so nothing wraps. The block rows are the phase's only pool accesses, so no element is touched by two invocations. The reader keeps an array transposed (batch 9) only when every write of it outside a solo phase is the simple block form `lidx * C + j`. An array also written another way, written in the grouped form `(lidx + k T) * C + j`, or longer than `T * C` keeps its natural layout. Its block writes then stand in block-write phases, its other phases (own writes, swap pairs) address it as any array, and its region starts at a multiple of `T * C` words (and of its swap span). Atomic stores and read-modify-writes of the atomic pool are allowed in the Y and E phases, since the atomic pool is not the plain pool.

## Proposal (not built): a fuel counter at run time, so every expressed kernel fits the per-submit cap (UI1, 2026-10-23, for Andrew)

**The problem, measured.** The work guard bounds one workgroup's node executions by the expressed tree (`tree_work_bound_workgroup_in`), and a dispatch over the per-submit cap (170 M) is split into submits of whole workgroups. A single workgroup over the cap cannot be split, and it does not run. The ingest run found this in 26 of 565 children (4.6 %); one leaf edit raised a loop literal from 4 to 32. A limit carried by the types alone cannot fix it. A by-construction bound must hold for the worst arrangement of every node, so at the run's program size it allows about one 64-trip loop a thread. On the 250k problem's largest fields, 11 of the 31 readers have a search workgroup bounded above the cap by today's count: the reference at 54×, run1 seed27 at 87×. A type-level limit would make our own index unexpressible.

**The proposal.** The rows stay as they are, and only the loop rows' spelling changes, so every saved gene still expresses.
1. **Two counters a thread.** The renderer declares `var fuel_u: u32` and `var fuel_x: u32` at kernel scope and initialises them from pipeline-overridable constants: `override FUEL_U: u32` and `override FUEL_X: u32`.
2. **The uniform counter.** A loop at an L rung, which may hold a barrier, charges `fuel_u`. The charge on each trip is the static node count of its body, outside the bodies of the loops nested in it. Every thread runs an L-rung loop under uniform control and charges the same constant, so `fuel_u` stays uniform. A loop that leaves on `fuel_u == 0` therefore leaves in every thread together, and no barrier is skipped by some threads only. naga's uniformity analysis has to accept this; that is test 1 below.
3. **The thread counter.** A loop at any other rung (C, W, E, Y, P, S, X, B) charges `fuel_x` with its own body's static cost. Such loops hold no barrier, so leaving one in some threads only is legal.
4. **The spelling.** The counted loop becomes `n = {bound}; kc = 0u; loop { k = kc; if (k >= n || fuel <= COST) { break; } fuel = fuel - COST; kc = k + step; {body} }`, where `COST` is a literal the renderer writes from the expressed body (the "declarations are row data" mechanism, applied to a per-node number). The form keeps the counted loop that Apple's compiler accepts, with no continuing block.
5. **Fuel a kernel.** The host sets `FUEL_U + FUEL_X = cap / T - S` at pipeline creation. `T` is the workgroup's threads (from the grid node) and `S` is the tree's straight-line node count outside all loops, so one workgroup's node executions are at most the cap by construction, whatever the gene. The work guard then always passes, and every child runs.
6. **The result when fuel runs out.** A kernel that runs out of fuel leaves its loops early and produces wrong values, which fitness scores like any other wrong values. The harness also reads a one-word flag that the loop sets when it leaves on fuel. The report counts those runs, so a child that is correct only on small cases is visible.

**The questions this proposal must answer before it is built.**
- **Overhead.** Each trip adds one compare and one subtract. For a loop body of a few nodes (the inner products of the search) that may cost 5 to 20 %. For bodies of tens of nodes it should be negligible. To measure: time the reference and three readers with and without the counter on the timing case, paired, as ST12's paired timing does.
- **Whether real work fits.** The type bounds above are upper bounds over every loop's cap, so the real trips of a search are far fewer. Whether today's reference stays correct under `FUEL = cap / T` is a measurement: run the instrumented reference and the 31 readers on the 250k case and count fuel exits per stage. Zero exits means the change costs only the overhead. Exits on the reference mean the cap itself is too small for the search on this machine, which is a hardware fact to report, not something the kingdom can fix.
- **Risks.** (a) Uniformity: naga must accept a break on `fuel_u` at L rungs. If it does not, the uniform counter has to come from a `workgroupUniformLoad` of a workgroup word, which costs a barrier on each trip. (b) The relaxed atomics already give order-dependent values; fuel exits add input-size-dependent truncation, so the champion's report must show fuel exits per case. (c) The renderer's per-node literal is new machinery: it must be the same for a gene and its write-back, which the round-trip tests check. (d) The Metal hang reproducer (docs/reports/2026-10-21_metal_hang_k2) must be re-run on the new loop form.
- **Fix, a race any gene could express (2026-10-23): reads clamped onto the guard word.** Every pool read inside a phase was clamped with `min(o, 6079u)`, onto the pool's last word, and that word is the own element of the thread whose index equals `6079 mod T`. With 128 threads, 6079 mod 128 = 63 is below a pair phase's `ps = 64`, so a partner load past the end read a word a writer of the phase owns. Own reads (W, X), swap-pair loads (E) and block loads (Y) past the end had the same flaw. Now `wg_pool` holds 6084 words; every write is still guarded by `o < 6080u`, so words 6080 to 6083 are never written, and every read clamps onto word 6080. The atomic pool has 2108 words, so the total is still 32,768 bytes; a workgroup variable is rounded to 16 bytes, which is why four words were added, not one. Only spellings changed, no rows; a running process keeps the old spelling until it restarts.
- **The pair phase over own elements `lidx + k T`.** `wg.pload_x_<e>_P: (register, u32.wgbase, u32.indexw k)` loads the partner of own element `lidx + k T`, at word `base + k T + lidx + ps`. Its residue modulo T is `lidx + ps`, which no writer of the phase has. The reader takes own writes and partners at any own element `lidx + k T` (`k` a literal or an expression), so `wred[256u + t] = wred[256u + t] + wred[256u + t + half]` stands in a pair phase.
- **The slot phase (A rung): slot claiming.** `wg.slot_L<d>_f<k>: (u32.wgbase sp, u32.slotc c, u32.xspan ss, block.A, block.L)` is spelled `workgroupBarrier(); { let sp = {0}; let sc = {1}; let ss = {2}; rs0 = 4294967295u; ... rs3 = 4294967295u; {3} } workgroupBarrier(); {4}`.
  - **The counters.** The slot counters are their own atomic array, `wg_scnt: array<atomic<u32>, 4>`; the atomic pool gives up four words and holds 2,104. Only three rows touch a counter: `slot.take_A: (reg<u32>.slot)`, spelled `{0} = atomicAdd(&wg_scnt[sc], 1u);`, which exists at A only; `slot.reset_<L|C|S>: (u32.slotc)`, spelled `atomicStore(&wg_scnt[{0}], 0u);`, which never stands in a slot phase; and `slot.count: (u32.slotc) -> u32.index`, a relaxed load with its `w` and `a` twins.
  - **Why every slot is distinct.** A slot register `rs0..rs3` (type `reg<u32>.slot`) is written only by a take, and is reset when a slot phase begins. In one phase every take uses the phase's one counter, and nothing else changes that counter in the phase. A workgroup makes fewer than 2^32 takes under the work guard. So every take in the phase returns a different slot.
  - **The writes and reads.** A write `wg.sset_<e>_A: (u32.xblk b, reg<u32>.slot, a value)` stores at `b * ss + s`, guarded by `s < ss && o < sp`. Two invocations' writes therefore never meet, whatever blocks a gene names. The phase reads the pool only through `wg.aload_<e>_A: (register, u32.wgbase, u32.indexa)`, at or above `sp`.
  - **The `a` family.** The phase's statements take the `a` value family: the twins of every value row except the workgroup reads, own reads included, so no value in the phase read an element below `sp`. Atomics of the atomic pool keep their twins, since they never race.
  - **The reader.** The reader takes a segment as a slot phase when every workgroup write stores at `L + s` (`L` a multiple of the span) of a slot `s` taken in the segment from one counter. The span is the smallest power of two above every bound its conditions prove on the slot, including the bound left after `if (s >= L) { break; }`. The slot arrays are placed below the arrays the phase reads. A counter is an atomic scalar whose `atomicAdd(&c, 1u)` names a write's element; its stores of 0 read as resets and its loads as counts.
- **The lane-split phase: the lanes below m run a split phase.** `wg.qsplit_L<d>_f<k>: (u32.wgbase sp, u32.lanes m, block.X, block.L)` is spelled `workgroupBarrier(); { let sp = {0}; let qm = min({1}, KNOB_THREADS); if (lidx < qm) { {2} } } workgroupBarrier(); {3}`. `m` is a bare power of two, so `qm` divides T. The X rung gains `wg.qset_<e>_X: (u32.wgbase, u32.qown, w value)`, which writes an active lane's own element `lidx + k qm` (`wg.qown_x`, `own.u32_qown = lidx`) below `sp`. The lanes below `qm` have distinct residues modulo `qm`, so no two of them write one element. A T-own element `lidx + k T` is `lidx + (k T / qm) qm`, so X's own writes and own reads stay their lane's own. The plain split phase now also binds `let qm = KNOB_THREADS;`, so `wg.qset` there is the T-own write; this is a spelling change only. The reader takes `if (lidx < m) { .. }` (with `m` a literal power of two below T) as a lane-split phase. Writes like `wcs[bb + t]` (with `bb` stepping by m) and `wprobe[NT + t]` are then own lane writes. seeds5b seed17 and seed19 read.
- **Strided loops that break, and uniform loops that break around a barrier.** The rows `loop.tile_strided_brk_<X>` and `loop.tilex_strided_brk_<c>_<X>` exist for X in L, C, W, S, X and A. A stride only lowers the trip count, so the class is the bound's. `block.ubrk_UB<d>_f<k>: (block.L, bool.uflag, block.L)` is a loop body that breaks on a uniform condition, so both of its parts keep the L rung and may hold barriers. It is bound by `loop.tile_ubrk_L`, `loop.tile_strided_ubrk_L`, `loop.tileu_ubrk_<c>_L` and `loop.tileu_strided_ubrk_<c>_L`, which bind the uniform counter, since every invocation leaves on the same trip. The reader takes the break flag `f = c; .. break if f` as `c` itself when nothing between them rewrites `f` or what `c` reads.
- **The pair phase at a granularity of qm lanes, and loops in pair phases.** `wg.qpair_L<d>_f<k>: (u32.uniform s, u32.lanes m, block.P, block.L)` is spelled with `let qm = min(m, T); let ps = min(s, qm / 2u);`. Writers below `ps` write their own elements `lidx + k qm` (`wg.qset_<e>_P`), read them (`wg.qload_<e>_P`), and load partners at `lidx + k qm + ps` (`wg.pload_q_<e>_P`). Written residues modulo `qm` lie below `ps`, and partner residues lie in `[ps, 2ps)`, which is within `qm`. The plain pair phase binds `qm = T` (spelling only). P gets W's loop rows. The reader loads a trip's partners and own reads at the top of the trip, and only reads an own element before the trip writes its array. `wf[b * 64u + t] = wf[b * 64u + t] + wf[b * 64u + t + s]` at 256 threads reads as a pair phase of 64-lane granularity (run1 seed30).
